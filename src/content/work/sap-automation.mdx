<Section label="The challenge" ruled>

Two jobs at Ortigas Land took people’s time without needing their judgement. Staff keyed hundreds of invoices, reversals and contract locks (holds on a property-sale contract while something is pending) into SAP by hand each month, and HR spent more than 100 hours a month processing government remittances: the statutory contributions an employer pays to Philippine government agencies. Both followed fixed rules, which made them good candidates for a bot.

</Section>

<Section label="The approach" ruled>

<FigRef n={1} />

### Use SAP the way staff do

The SAP bot drives the same desktop client staff use and follows the same steps a person would, so SAP itself didn’t have to change to be automated. When a screen isn’t what the bot expects, it stops, logs the item and leaves it for a person instead of guessing.

<FigRef n={2} />

### Read the documents, then a person approves

The remittance bot uses AI document extraction to read the figures from HR’s source documents and does the keying. HR reviews the extracted figures and approves them before anything is paid, so the bot saves the typing, not the judgement.

### How the savings were measured

Staff timed the manual process before the bots went live. Afterwards, the bots’ run logs and the manual time that remained gave the comparison behind both figures.

### Lead the team that builds them

I led a four-person development team and introduced structured code reviews and secure development practices alongside the automation work.

</Section>

<Section label="The result" ruled>

Manual SAP processing time fell by 70–80% across hundreds of entries a month, and more than 100 hours a month of HR keying moved to the remittance bot, with HR still approving every payment.

</Section>
